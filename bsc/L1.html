🧠 2. Knowledge vs Intelligence

These two words sound similar, but they are not the same thing:

📚 Knowledge

Facts you have stored. Like knowing all the state capitals by heart.

💡 Intelligence

The ability to use what you know to solve a new problem you've never faced before.

Example: Your GPS suddenly stops working. Knowledge doesn't help here — you need intelligence to figure out the way using landmarks, memory, and common sense.

So... the auto driver? Intelligent. The topper who freezes without notes? Full of knowledge, but not using intelligence.

🤖 3. So What Is "Artificial" Intelligence?

Artificial

Man-made, not natural

Intelligence

The ability to think, solve problems, adapt to new situations

Artificial Intelligence = teaching machines to do the "figuring out" part — not just storing facts, but solving new problems.

🏛️ 4. Where Did the Name Come From?

In 1956, a group of scientists met for a summer workshop at Dartmouth College, USA.

One of them, John McCarthy, needed a name for this brand-new field. He simply joined two everyday English words:

Artificial + Intelligence

That's it — no secret formula. Just two simple words describing exactly what it means: man-made thinking.

🗺️ 8. How AI Actually "Figures Things Out"

Think of a treasure hunt:

🙈 No Clues

You check every single room in the house — slow, tiring, blind guessing.

🧭 With Clues

"Cold near the kitchen, warm near the bedroom" — you search smartly and quickly.

AI works exactly like this: it searches for the answer, guided by clues (knowledge). No clues = slow guessing. Good clues = fast, smart answers.

🕵️ 9. Can Machines Actually "Think"? — Turing Test

In 1950, scientist Alan Turing asked a simple question: "Can machines think?" He designed a simple test — you chat with someone through text, not knowing if it's a human or a machine. If you can't tell the difference, the machine "passes."

Ever chatted with a customer-care bot and wondered "wait, is this a real person?" — that's the Turing Test happening to you, live.

🕰️ 10. A Quick, Fun History of AI

1956
The name "Artificial Intelligence" is born at Dartmouth College.
1980s
AI Winter — people got over-excited, results didn't match promises, funding dropped.
1997
A computer named Deep Blue beats the world chess champion.
2010s–today
The AI boom we live in — voice assistants, self-driving cars, chatbots.
Lesson: AI has had ups and downs — it's not a straight success story, and that's completely okay.

  • AI = Sense → Think → Act, guided by clues (knowledge) to search smartly.
