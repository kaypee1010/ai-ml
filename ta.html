AI Tool Usage Policy

You can

  • Use AI for learning & brainstorming ("Explain how convolutional layers work")
  • Use AI for boilerplate code ("Generate a Flask app template with 3 routes")
  • Use AI to debug ("Why does my model return NaN during training?")

You can't

  • Copy code blocks and submit as-is without understanding every line
  • Ask AI to write your entire training script, then submit it unchanged
  • Use AI-generated paper text without rewriting it in your own words
  • Rely on AI to do your thinking — you must understand your own results

At viva you must explain: every algorithm you used and why, every major code function, what your results mean, and your individual contribution. If you can't explain it, don't submit it. Turnitin target: <20% similarity, with proper citations — if you use an AI tool, acknowledge it in the paper.

Week-by-Week Success Tips

  • Weeks 1–2: Lock in team and topic early; start reading papers immediately
  • Weeks 2–4: Understand your dataset inside-out — clean, visualize, summarize
  • Weeks 4–6: Build a baseline model fast. Aim for "working," not "perfect" — you'll iterate
  • Weeks 7–8: Mid-term — you should have results, even imperfect ones
  • Weeks 9–12: Experiment with algorithms/hyperparameters/features; document every run
  • Weeks 13–14: Start writing the paper — draft one section per day, don't wait for Week 15
  • Week 15: Polish presentation, rehearse the demo, know your project inside-out

Frequently Asked Questions

What if I don't know Python well?
Learn as you go — tutorials, peers, and faculty office hours are all there to help.
Can we change our topic after Week 1?
No. Choose wisely. If you truly dislike your topic, talk to faculty early (Week 2–3).
Our accuracy is only 60% — will we fail?
Not necessarily. A 60% model with deep analysis of why it fails beats an 85% model with no analysis.
Can we use a pre-trained model like BERT or ResNet?
Yes — transfer learning is valid. Document what you changed and what you trained from scratch.
Is it OK to adapt a solution we found on GitHub?
You can study and adapt it — never copy-paste. You must understand and modify every line.
What if one teammate does nothing?
At viva, each student is asked individually what they did. If someone can't explain their part, they're marked down individually.
