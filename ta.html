FAQs

Can our team mix sections?
Yes. Any four or five students from 7A, 7B, 7C or 7D can register together.
We registered but the page closed before we picked a topic. What now?
Use the Continue box at the top of the Register tab. The leader enters their registration number and the team goes straight back to track selection.
Can we change topic after confirming?
No. Read the full topic brief before you confirm — that is what the detail view is for.
Can two teams take the same topic?
No. All 69 topics are unique across the cohort, so once a topic is confirmed it is gone for everyone.
Can we use ChatGPT or Claude?
Yes, for learning and brainstorming. You must understand and be able to explain every line at the viva. Blind copying is an automatic fail.
Can we use a public dataset?
Yes. Cite the source and explain why you chose it.
Our topic needs human participants. What then?
Get written informed consent and institutional ethics clearance before collecting anything. Build this into Week 2-3, not Week 12.

Reporting results honestly

Every number needs an interval A bare accuracy figure is not a result. Report a confidence interval or a variance across seeds, and compare against a stated baseline. Two models at 85 and 87 percent are often statistically indistinguishable, and claiming an improvement you cannot defend is the single most common weakness in submitted papers.
  • State your baseline before reporting your model
  • Report variability across random seeds, not a single best run
  • Show where the model fails, not only where it succeeds
  • Match the metric to the problem — accuracy misleads on imbalanced data
  • Keep test data untouched until the final evaluation

AI tool policy

You can

  • Use AI for learning and brainstorming ("explain how convolutional layers work")
  • Use AI for boilerplate ("generate a Flask template with three routes")
  • Use AI to debug ("why does my model return NaN during training?")

You cannot

  • Copy code blocks and submit them without understanding every line
  • Have AI write your training script and submit it unchanged
  • Use AI-generated paper text without rewriting it in your own words
  • Rely on AI to do your thinking — you must understand your own results

At the viva you must explain every algorithm and why you chose it, every major function, what your results mean, and your individual contribution. If you cannot explain it, do not submit it. Turnitin target is under 20 percent with proper citation, and any AI tool you used should be acknowledged in the paper.

Research ethics

  • Human participants require written informed consent and ethics clearance before collection
  • Participants may withdraw at any point, and their data goes with them
  • Institutional data must be anonymised before modelling — hashed identifiers, contact and medical fields removed
  • State dataset provenance plainly, including when data is simulated rather than observed
  • Any system touching people needs a written account of who is harmed when it is wrong

Week by week

  • Weeks 1-2: Lock in the team and topic, start reading papers immediately
  • Weeks 2-4: Understand your dataset thoroughly — clean, visualise, summarise
  • Weeks 4-6: Build a baseline fast. Aim for working, not perfect
  • Weeks 7-8: Mid-term. You should have results, even imperfect ones
  • Weeks 9-12: Experiment with algorithms, hyperparameters and features. Log every run
  • Weeks 13-14: Write the paper, one section per day. Do not wait for Week 15
  • Week 15: Polish slides, rehearse the demo, know the project thoroughly

FAQs

I do not know Python well. What now?
Learn as you go. Tutorials, peers and office hours are all available, and Week 1-4 exists for exactly this.
Our accuracy is only 60 percent. Will we fail?
No. A 60 percent model with a real analysis of why it fails beats an 85 percent model with none.
Can we use a pre-trained model such as BERT or ResNet?
Yes, transfer learning is valid. Document what you changed and what you trained from scratch.
Can we adapt a solution found on GitHub?
You can study and adapt it, never copy-paste. You must understand and be able to modify every line.
What if a teammate does nothing?
Each student is questioned individually at the viva. Someone who cannot explain their part is marked down individually.

Before you submit

  • Code runs end to end without errors; requirements.txt complete; commits meaningful
  • Paper: 6-8 pages IEEE format, 10+ references all cited, under 20 percent Turnitin
  • Every reported metric carries an interval or a variance across seeds
  • Slides: 10-12, font 18pt or larger, demo script or recorded backup ready
  • Every member can explain their individual contribution
  • Faculty has repository access, shared if private
